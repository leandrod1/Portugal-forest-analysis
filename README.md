# 🌲 Portugal Forest Composition & Wildfire Dynamics (1995–2025)
*A 30-Year Geospatial & Socio-Economic Data Analysis*

---

### 📖 Abstract & Executive Summary

Over the last three decades, Portugal has experienced a severe increase in the frequency, extent, and severity of wildfires. Public debate often points to the rapid expansion of fast-growing **Eucalyptus (*Eucalyptus globulus*)** as a primary catalyst. However, this study evaluates whether eucalyptus plantations act as the sole driver of wildfires, or if fire regimes are better explained by broader environmental, spatial, and socio-economic dynamics.

By integrating official geospatial land cover maps (**COS**) and historical wildfire registries (**ICNF**), this project examines the evolution of Portugal's forest composition, the economic importance of the forestry sector, and the spatial relationship between tree species distribution and burned area patterns.

### 🔬 Research Questions & Hypotheses

* **Structural Shift:** How has Portugal’s forest cover transitioned between 1995 and 2023, particularly regarding Eucalyptus?
* **Socio-Economic Drivers:** To what extent do industrial economic incentives (pulp & paper exports) drive the expansion of monocultures?
* **Fire Association:** Do eucalyptus monocultures exhibit a disproportionately higher fire frequency compared to other forest types, or do they share similar burned area proportions with species like Maritime Pine?

---

### 💡 Key Findings

* **Species Displacement:** Eucalyptus has expanded from ~70,000 hectares in the mid-20th century to over 965,000 hectares, becoming the most widespread tree species in mainland Portugal and systematically replacing historical maritime pine areas.
* **Economic Footprint:** The forestry sector represents ~8% of Portugal's total national exports (over 51% driven by paper, cardboard, and pulp industries), explaining the continuous investment in fast-growing species.
* **Fire Regime Insights:** 
  * Multifactorial Drivers: Wildfire occurrence in Portugal is strongly influenced by systemic factors, including land abandonment, fuel accumulation, extreme weather conditions, and forest management practices.
  * Cause vs. Propagation: While eucalyptus should not be considered the sole cause of wildfires in Portugal, its biological characteristics and large-scale monoculture expansion contribute significantly to wildfire propagation under specific conditions.
  * Forest Diversification: Transitioning toward more diverse forest systems, incorporating native species like oak and chestnut, is essential to enhancing landscape resilience.
  * Management & Policy: Combining active land management with targeted economic support for landowners serves as a critical lever to effectively mitigate and reduce wildfire risk across the territory.
